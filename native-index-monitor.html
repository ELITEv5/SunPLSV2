Can SunPLS become PulseChain's native monetary unit?

This page started as a live theoretical test — two new purchasing power oracles running alongside the deployed SunPLS protocol. It's grown into a full research dashboard centered on one question: can SunPLS carry functional purchasing power forward better than today's R-only mechanism? A regime classifier, a live attack-cost and arbitrage model, and the SunPLS Purchasing Power Simulator all exist to answer that honestly before anything touches real monetary policy — a bonus PLS-timing backtest is included too, clearly marked as a side experiment, not the thesis. No code has changed on the live protocol. Nothing is wired into the controller yet. We are watching the data — and now, modeling what would happen if we did.

What is SunPLS

SunPLS is a WPLS-backed CDP protocol on PulseChain. Lock WPLS as collateral, mint SunPLS. A stability fee and redemption mechanism keeps the market price close to the redemption price R — a target the market is expected to chase, not the other way around. The protocol is live, immutable, and fully on-chain.

Oracle 1 — Native Asset Basket Index

Tracks a basket of PulseChain-native assets denominated in WPLS. Starts at 1.0× and is capped at 1% movement per update — slow and deliberate. Every price comes from PulseX V1 on-chain liquidity. No Chainlink. No external feeds. The chain prices itself.

This is a price-heat reference, not the purchasing-power signal — it tracks how strong demand for PulseChain's own assets is, which is a different question from whether functional access (Oracle 3) is actually getting cheaper or more expensive. The two frequently diverge in the live data — native running hot on asset appreciation while functional access stays flat or falls — and that divergence is the whole point: it's the evidence for why a policy based on native price alone would chase asset speculation rather than real usage.

Oracle 2 — Functional Index v2 (calibrated trade sizes)

Measures three economic functions on PulseChain: blockspace cost (20%), market access slippage (40%), and liquidity depth (40%). Trade sizes are derived from live WPLS pool reserves at deploy time, targeting ~0.5% initial slippage: PLSX/WPLS ≈ 509M WPLS, INC/WPLS ≈ 155M WPLS. Collateral access (SunPLS/WPLS) was removed — it was circular. All three components respond to real pool conditions. Runs as the raw-blockspace reference alongside the EMA version.

Oracle 3 — Functional Index v4 (EMA blockspace + EMA reserves)

Fixes v2's blockspace volatility. Live data showed gas swings dominating the accepted index (oscillating 0.9872x ↔ 1.0100x from gas alone). v3 applied a 10% EMA to the gas price:

smoothedGas = 0.10 × block.basefee + 0.90 × prevSmoothedGas

A single gas spike contributes only 10% to the smoothed value. Sustained high gas builds up over ~10 updates before reaching 65% of its full effect. The gasSnapshot() function exposes both smoothed and raw gas side-by-side so the monitor can show exactly how much the EMA is dampening vs what raw gas would give. Same weights as v2.

v3's market access and liquidity depth were still read live from pool reserves — manipulable within a single block (quantified: ~$257 via flash loan to flip a full update between the 1% cap's two extremes, against PulseChain's actual ~$1.76M total lending TVL). v4 replaces the live reserve reads with the same EMA smoothing gas already used, on the reserves themselves — a single-block manipulation now only carries ~10% of its size into the index instead of 100%. v3 is retired; the dashboard reads v4 exclusively.

A separate health overlay reads the live vault, stability pool, and price oracle. It can slow or freeze index influence on monetary policy — but it never directly raises R.

Core Access Regime

A Buy / Hold / Caution classifier built on top of the two functional oracles — analytics only, not policy. Compares Native (asset heat) against Functional v4 (access cost) against four threshold rules, each carrying a Mild/Strong qualifier based on how far past the threshold a reading actually sits — "0.9965x" and "0.954x" used to look identical on screen; now they don't.

Health must read degraded continuously for 90 seconds before a health-driven Caution is trusted — the live oracle's health can blip on a single tick, and a real sustained problem looks different from a moment of refresh-state noise. Below that threshold the panel shows Data Pending instead: an honest "not confirmed yet," never an instant flip to alarm.

Observation Log & PLS Trading Signal Experiment

Every real update() writes one row to the Observation Log — native, functional, health, regime, market price P, and PLS price at that moment. This is the actual dataset the whole page exists to build, growing at roughly 1-3 rows a day.

The PLS Trading Signal Experiment further down the page (clearly marked "Not SunPLS policy") is a bonus paper-trading backtest of the regime signal, priced against the real on-chain PulseX WPLS/DAI pair. It answers one narrow question — can the regime signal time PLS entries/exits — which is a different question from the actual SunPLS thesis (can SunPLS carry functional purchasing power forward). Buys and sells only on a confirmed signal — 2 consecutive real update() events plus a 24-hour minimum hold — never on staleness or a health blip alone; missing or degraded data is never itself a sell signal, only a genuine bearish economic reading is. An earlier version got this wrong (could exit easily but only re-enter on a much narrower condition; caught in the live data as an 18-day stretch stuck in cash through a rally) and was fixed.

Manipulation Cost, Redemption Risk & Arbitrage Opportunity

Three panels answering the security and arbitrage questions directly, live, from the deployed contracts' real reserves — not one-time estimates that go stale. Manipulation Cost: how much would it cost to move v4's accepted index by 1bp / 10bp / 100bp, per route. Redemption Risk: even if someone did, how much could actually be extracted? SunPLS's redeem() only pays out against vaults already undercollateralized (CR ≤ 130%, verified directly in the Vault source) — so extractable value is bounded by how much distressed debt is sitting in the protocol at that moment, not by total TVL. A Stress Scenarios grid shows hypothetical distressed-debt × price-gap combinations, since today's protocol (currently one vault) is too small to exercise the real numbers. Arbitrage Opportunity makes explicit what redemption risk alone doesn't say: no redemption edge (P ≥ R) does not mean no arbitrage edge — when P > R there's a separate, normal issuance/mint arb (mint SunPLS against WPLS collateral, sell above R), which isn't an attack surface the way redemption extraction is, since minting is CR-gated rather than bounded by distressed protocol debt.

SunPLS Purchasing Power Simulator

The actual question this page exists to answer: can SunPLS carry functional purchasing power forward better than today's R-only mechanism, or a naive native-index- direct design? Replays the real Observation Log — not synthetic data — through three transmission models starting from today's live R: no index influence (baseline), native-direct tracking (the design we rejected, kept only as the comparison case), and v4 slow bias — capped(index − 1.0), max 0.25%/day, requires health = 100% and 3 consecutive confirmed updates in the same direction. Writes nothing on-chain. Each scenario reports reference value retained vs today (R drift only — a lower hypothetical R isn't necessarily worse if v4's own index also shows access getting cheaper, so this is not yet a true functional-purchasing-power outcome), and whether its hypothetical R would currently put the protocol under redemption pressure (P below R) or issuance pressure (P above R) against today's live market price — historical P wasn't logged before 2026-08-13, so this is evaluated against today's P, not a full historical replay yet. True functional purchasing power retained needs a historical V4 + historical P/R replay; new observations now record P so both become possible going forward.

The Hypothesis

What if R tracked functional purchasing power? As PulseChain grows — more chain usage, deeper liquidity, rising gas demand — the index rises and R rises with it. SunPLS holders would not hold a static peg. They would hold a claim on PulseChain's own economic function, appreciating slowly as the ecosystem expands.

This is fundamentally different from every stablecoin today. Not pegged to USD. Not pegged to gold. Not a token index. Pegged to measurable economic activity on the chain it lives on — and still fully collateralised and redeemable at all times.

Scoped honestly, on purpose: SunPLS does not attempt to preserve purchasing power against the dollar or the external world. It attempts to preserve functional purchasing power inside PulseChain, using on-chain measurements of blockspace, market access, and liquidity depth. There is no external reference anywhere in this system — no oracle, no dollar, nothing outside the chain is ever consulted. That's deliberate, and it's also the honest limit of the claim: this design can tell you whether access inside PulseChain is getting cheaper or more expensive, not whether PulseChain's value in the outside world is rising or falling.

What we're watching

  • Do both indices move in ways that are economically meaningful — tracking real PulseChain conditions rather than noise?
  • Do the two indices agree or diverge? Persistent divergence tells us something important about what each one actually measures — confirmed live: native ripping while v4 stays near par, repeatedly.
  • Does the P / Native R ratio stay close to 100%? That would mean the market would naturally find equilibrium under this regime.
  • Does the functional index give different information than the asset basket — or is it just a noisier version of the same signal?
  • Are borrowers and holders not destabilised by slow, capped index movement?
  • Does the PLS Trading Signal Experiment actually outperform buy-and-hold, now that the entry/exit asymmetry is fixed? Still an open, unanswered question — the old number no longer applies, and there's no new verdict yet. This is a side curiosity, not the thesis.
  • Does the Manipulation Cost / Redemption Risk safety margin hold as the protocol grows, or only because today's deployment is nearly empty?
  • Does the SunPLS Purchasing Power Simulator show v4-bias actually retaining more functional purchasing power than baseline or native-direct, once there's enough replayed history to say so with confidence?

What success looks like — and where this actually stands

Over weeks and months both indices drift slowly and meaningfully. P / Native R stays close to par. The functional index provides signal that the asset basket misses. The health overlay behaves correctly under stress. So far, live data backs all of this up.

That's necessary, not sufficient. Before anything gets wired into a real Controller v2: a historical backtest (not just live accumulation going forward), a finished transmission design (the Purchasing Power Simulator answers "what would happen," not yet "what should the exact cap be"), and evidence the manipulation-cost/redemption-risk safety margin holds at real scale, not just on a nearly-empty test deployment. If all of that holds, Controller v2 wires v4 in as a parallel deploy alongside the live protocol — never a mutation of it. SunPLS becomes the first CDP asset with a monetary policy set by the chain it lives on — not by a dollar peg, not by token prices, but by what the chain can do.

Oracle Poke / Refresh  ·  manual research maintenance, not autonomy
One click, same sequence as the manual workflow: triggers the Controller epoch, then pokes Native / Functional v2 / V4 in turn, then does a final refresh. Still one wallet confirmation per step underneath — this automates the clicking and waiting between them, not the signing. If the epoch isn't due yet, that step is skipped as expected, not treated as an error.
For the clearest picture, poke Native Index, Functional v2, and V4 Oracle together, in one sitting, before relying on the divergence/comparison metrics elsewhere on this page (Core Access Regime, the Purchasing Power Simulator, Arbitrage Opportunity). Those compare the oracles against each other — if one was last poked hours ago and another minutes ago, an apparent divergence can just be a timing mismatch, not a real signal. The Controller poke below is independent of this: `triggerEpoch()` reads a separate, unrelated price oracle (verified in source), so its order relative to the three above doesn't affect their freshness — poke it on its own schedule, whenever you specifically want to test or advance the live rate mechanism.
